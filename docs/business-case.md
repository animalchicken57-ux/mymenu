# Business Case: MyMenu

*Prepared for: the investor conversation • Date: 2026-08-09*

This document answers the four questions an investor actually asks: how big is
the problem, how does this make money, what are you asking me for, and what do I
get back. [product-brief.md](product-brief.md) covers the idea itself and does
not repeat here.

Every figure below is sourced at the bottom. Where a number is our assumption
rather than a published fact, it says so.

---

## 1. The ask

**AED 250,000 for 15% of the company.** That values MyMenu at AED 1.67 million
post-money.

The money buys twelve months, and it is almost all spent on the one thing the
software cannot do by itself — walking into restaurants.

| What | Cost |
|---|---:|
| One field salesperson, 12 months @ 8,000 | 96,000 |
| Part-time Arabic-speaking support and onboarding, 12 months @ 4,000 | 48,000 |
| Advertising — search and Instagram, aimed at the segment in §3 | 30,000 |
| Trade licence, legal, accounting | 30,000 |
| Card payment integration (one-off build) | 25,000 |
| Printed QR table stands, demo materials | 12,000 |
| Contingency | 6,000 |
| Infrastructure and tools, 12 months | 3,000 |
| **Total** | **250,000** |

Nothing in that table is software. The product is built; this is the cost of
putting it in front of people.

**The milestone this buys:** 200 paying restaurants by month 12 — AED 60,000
monthly recurring revenue, AED 720,000 annualised.

### If you would rather buy it outright

Offered because it is a fair question, not because it is the preferred answer:
**AED 750,000 for the whole company** — code, brand, domain, customers — plus
**AED 8,000 a month** to keep it running and fix what breaks. That retainer buys
maintenance, not a roadmap; new features are quoted separately.

750,000 is roughly one year of the plan's revenue. On the plan in §6, 15% is
worth AED 2.2–3.2M by year three, so the outright price is deliberately set
where selling the whole thing is the worse deal for us — and, if the plan holds,
the better one for the buyer only if they can sell faster than we can.

## 2. The problem, in money

A UAE restaurant selling through a delivery app pays **15% to 30% commission** to
Talabat and **25% to 35%** to Deliveroo.<sup>[1][2]</sup> Industry analysis puts
the *real* take rate 8 to 12 points above the headline number once service fees,
featured placement and peak-hour surcharges are counted.<sup>[1]</sup>

Restaurant operators have said so publicly for years:

> "Aggregators charge you up to 35 per cent on an order... it is a sizable
> portion of your revenue that you are giving away."
> — Bhanu Pratap Rathore, GrowthX Advisors<sup>[3]</sup>

> "Not only that, but they have a lot of other fees that they charge restaurant
> owners."
> — Gaurav Varma, CEO, Royal Orchid Group<sup>[3]</sup>

*(Source [3] is from 2020. It is quoted because it is a named UAE operator
speaking on record; the 2026 rate figures in [1] and [2] confirm the range has
not moved.)*

**What that costs one restaurant.** A restaurant doing AED 30,000 a month in
delivery, at 25%, hands over AED 7,500 a month — **AED 90,000 a year** — and
still finishes the year not knowing one customer's name.

## 3. Who we sell to

"Restaurants" is not a segment. There are two different people here, and the one
who pays is not the one who taps.

### The buyer — our customer

| | |
|---|---|
| Who | The owner of a one-branch cafeteria. His own name is on the trade licence |
| Age | 30–50 |
| Business | Shawarma and grill, curry house, karak café, small Arabic kitchen. One to three branches, never a chain |
| Where | Deira, Karama, Al Quoz, Al Nahda, Al Majaz — not the Marina or DIFC, which are hotel outlets and franchises |
| Turnover | AED 20,000–60,000 a month, a third to a half of it through an aggregator |
| Staff | No IT person, no marketing person, no procurement. He decides in one conversation |
| Tools | WhatsApp is the CRM. Instagram is the marketing. Maybe a cheap till |
| Already spends on | An aggregator listing, printed menus, boosted Instagram posts |
| Language | Arabic or English, and the product must be Arabic-first rather than Arabic-translated |
| Finds us by searching | "talabat commission rate", "نسبة عمولة طلبات", "QR menu Dubai", "online ordering system restaurant UAE" |

Those searches matter more than they look: they are high-intent, low-volume and
cheap, and they are the entire paid-acquisition plan. We are not buying "food
delivery" as a keyword.

### The user — his customer

18–40, lives in the UAE, orders from a phone, men and women equally. Critically
they are **returning, not discovering** — they already chose this restaurant, so
we never have to solve demand generation. About half read Arabic first. None of
them will install an app for one shawarma shop, which is why ordering is a web
page and not an app.

**This profile is our own segmentation, not a published statistic.** The market
sizes below are sourced; this is the judgement applied to them.

## 4. The market

The UAE online food delivery market was worth **USD 2.51 billion in 2024**
(≈ AED 9.2 billion) and is forecast to reach **USD 3.96 billion by 2030**, a 7.4%
compound annual growth rate.<sup>[4]</sup> The commission problem grows with it.

There are **over 26,795 restaurants and cafés in the UAE**.<sup>[5]</sup> Dubai
alone has 8,617 restaurants and 5,240 coffee shops and cafeterias.<sup>[5]</sup>

At AED 300 a month:

| | Restaurants | Annual revenue |
|---|---:|---:|
| **TAM** — every restaurant and café in the UAE | 26,795 | AED 96.5M |
| **SAM** — small independents, our assumption of ~45%<sup>†</sup> | ~12,000 | AED 43.2M |
| **SOM** — target by end of year three | 1,000 | AED 3.6M |

<sup>†</sup> Our estimate, not a published figure. Hotel outlets, international
chains and franchise groups are excluded — they buy enterprise POS systems, not
a 300 AED page. 1,000 restaurants is **3.7% of the total market**.

## 5. How MyMenu makes money

**AED 300 per restaurant per month, flat.** No commission, no per-order fee, no
percentage. The first 30 orders each month are free so a restaurant can try it
properly.

**AED 500 one-off**, optional, if they want us to type their menu in for them.
This is not a real revenue line — it is a way to remove the only reason a busy
owner puts off starting.

**Why flat and not a percentage.** A percentage is the thing the customer is
running away from. If we took a cut, we would start wanting their bills to be
bigger, and we would end up being exactly what they left.

### The break-even the customer cares about

At a 25% commission rate, AED 300 is what a restaurant pays on **AED 1,200** of
orders. At an average order of AED 60, that is **20 orders a month — fewer than
one a day.** Past that, every order is pure saving.

That is the entire sales conversation, and the product runs the counter itself:
the owner's dashboard shows what they kept this month, every time they log in.

### Unit economics

| | |
|---|---:|
| Revenue per restaurant | AED 300 / month |
| Infrastructure cost per restaurant at scale | under AED 10 / month |
| Gross margin (after support time and payment fees) | ~90% |
| Target cost to acquire one restaurant | AED 400 |
| **Payback period** | **1.4 months** |
| Assumed monthly churn | 5% |
| Implied customer lifetime | 20 months |
| **Lifetime value** | **AED 5,400** |
| **LTV / CAC** | **13.5×** |

Churn and CAC are planning assumptions, not measured results. They are the two
numbers to check first with real customers.

## 6. Three-year plan

| | Restaurants | MRR | ARR |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| End of year 1 | 200 | AED 60,000 | AED 720,000 |
| End of year 2 | 500 | AED 150,000 | AED 1,800,000 |
| End of year 3 | 1,000 | AED 300,000 | AED 3,600,000 |

Year one assumes one salesperson signing roughly 17 restaurants a month. That is
under one a working day, for a product that costs less than a part-time waiter
and takes a minute to decide on. **Sales pace is the main risk in this plan, not
the software** — the software is built.

## 7. The competition, honestly

**Someone has already proved this model works in this market.**

**ChatFood** (founded 2018, Dubai) sold exactly this: direct ordering, order-and-
pay at the table, flat monthly fee, no commission. It processed **over AED 100
million in direct orders** and saved restaurants an estimated **AED 30 million**
in commission, running at over 300 outlets.<sup>[6][7]</sup> **Deliverect
acquired ChatFood in May 2023.**<sup>[6]</sup>

**Ordable** offers branded direct-ordering sites at 0% commission across the
Gulf.<sup>[8]</sup>

This is the strongest slide in the deck, not the weakest:

1. **The market is proven.** We are not asking an investor to believe a theory. A
   company did this here and was bought for it.
2. **There is a known exit.** ChatFood is the comparable.
3. **The acquisition left a gap.** ChatFood is now part of Deliverect, an
   enterprise platform sold to chains and aggregator-integrated groups. The
   one-branch shawarma place is not their customer any more.

**Where MyMenu differs:** we serve only the small independent. One to three
branches, non-technical, no POS system, no IT person. Set up in an afternoon,
priced at one number a busy owner can decide on in a minute, and Arabic-first
rather than Arabic-translated. We are deliberately not building the enterprise
product, because that market already has three of them.

## 8. What the investor gets

**Return on the plan above.** SaaS businesses of this size trade at roughly 4–6×
annual recurring revenue.

| | ARR | Valuation at 4–6× | Value of 15% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| End of year 1 | 720,000 | 2.9M – 4.3M | 432,000 – 648,000 |
| End of year 3 | 3,600,000 | 14.4M – 21.6M | 2.2M – 3.2M |

On AED 250,000, that is roughly **1.7× to 2.6× within eighteen months**, and
**9× to 13× by year three**, if the plan holds.

**Three ways the money comes back:**

1. **Acquisition.** The precedent already exists — Deliverect bought ChatFood.
   Regional POS companies buying distribution is the normal pattern here.
2. **Cash.** At 90% gross margin this throws off money once sales cost is
   covered. It does not require an exit to pay.
3. **Roll-up.** Menu, orders and customer data in one place is the foundation for
   loyalty, re-engagement and stock alerts — each one sellable to the same
   installed base at no new acquisition cost.

## 9. What could go wrong

Stated plainly, because an investor will find these anyway.

1. **Sales, not software, is the bottleneck.** The product is built and works.
   Nothing in this plan is achieved without someone in a car visiting
   restaurants. This is what the money is for, and it is the assumption most
   likely to be wrong.
2. **Better-funded competitors exist.** Deliverect and Ordable can outspend us.
   Our answer is a segment they have priced themselves out of, not a feature.
3. **The delivery apps could cut commissions.** Unlikely at scale — commission is
   their business model — but a serious cut would narrow our saving. Note that it
   would not touch dine-in and pickup, where the apps do not operate at all.
4. **A very small restaurant may not save money.** Under roughly 20 orders a
   month through MyMenu, AED 300 costs more than the commission avoided. We
   should not sell to those restaurants, and the free first 30 orders means they
   find out before they pay.
5. **Churn is unmeasured.** 5% monthly is an assumption. If the real figure is
   10%, lifetime value halves and the CAC target has to halve with it.
