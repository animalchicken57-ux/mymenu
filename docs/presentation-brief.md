## What changed, and why

**Slide 4 — the segment.** The deck had market *size* (26,795 restaurants) and
called that the market. Size is not a segment. The brief asks who, in detail:
age, language, where they are, what they already buy, what they search for. The
slide now names one buyer — the owner of a one-branch cafeteria, 30 to 50, own
name on the licence, Deira or Karama or Al Nahda, AED 20–60k a month, runs the
business on WhatsApp — and separately names the diner who actually taps the
screen, because confusing the two is the standard way a B2B pitch goes wrong.

It also cuts the claimed market from 26,795 to about 12,000, which is the
honest number: hotel outlets and franchises buy enterprise systems, not a
300-dirham page.

The persona is our own segmentation, not a published statistic, and the slide
says so. The market and commission figures on the other slides are sourced, and
those sources are printed on the last slide.

**Slide 6 — the alternatives.** Previously one line: "I am not fighting
Talabat." True, but it answers a question nobody asked. It is now a table of
what the owner could actually buy instead — the aggregators, ChatFood (bought by
Deliverect), Ordable, Foodics and the POS systems, and a QR code that opens a
PDF — each with why it does not fit a one-branch kitchen. The "not fighting
Talabat" line survives as the closing note, which is where it belongs.

**Slide 11 — the ask.** Previously "250,000 for 15%" with one line about a
salesperson. The brief asks what the money is *for* — hiring, advertising,
customer service — and what the investor gets, and for how long. The slide now
itemises all 250,000 (96k salesperson, 48k Arabic support, 30k advertising, 76k
licence, card payments, table stands and hosting), states that the 15% is equity
kept permanently rather than a cut of profit for a year, and names the
alternative deal shape the brief raises: AED 750,000 to buy it outright plus AED
8,000 a month to keep it running.

Those four figures are the same ones in `business-case.md` §1, grouped for a
slide. If one changes, change both — an investor who adds up the slide and the
document and gets two answers stops trusting the rest.

Offering both is the point. The brief asks which one you want; answering with
one and refusing to price the other reads as not having thought about it.

## Timing

8 minutes talking · 5 minutes demo · 2 minutes questions. The deck's own clock
is set to 15.

If it runs long, shorten slide 9 (market size) to a sentence — slide 4 already
does the important half of that argument. Slides 4 and 11 are the two the brief
names directly and must not be cut.

## The demo, deliberately short

Four steps: scan, order, cook it, watch the savings counter move. Nothing else
gets opened unless asked. The menu editor, customer list, order history,
settings and drivers page all exist and all work — saying "it is all there, ask
me and I will open it" is stronger than spending the last two minutes clicking
through them.
