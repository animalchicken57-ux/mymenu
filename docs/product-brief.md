# Product Brief: MyMenu

*Prepared for: investor conversation • Date: 2026-08-05*

---

## Executive Summary

A small restaurant in Saudi Arabia sells a 60 SAR meal through a delivery app and
keeps roughly 42 of it. The rest goes to commission. The restaurant never learns
who the customer was, cannot contact them again, and has no way to bring them
back except by paying the same commission a second time. The restaurant is
renting its own customers.

**MyMenu gives every restaurant its own ordering page.** Its own menu, its own
QR code on the table, its own order screen in the kitchen, and — the part that
matters — its own customer list. The restaurant keeps the full 60 SAR and pays
MyMenu a flat 300 SAR a month, no matter how many orders come through.

For a restaurant doing 500 orders a month, that is roughly **9,000 SAR of
commission replaced by a 300 SAR bill.** The product proves this itself: the
owner's dashboard runs a live counter showing how much commission they avoided
this month. The sales pitch is a number on their own screen.

## The Problem

**The commission.** Delivery platforms in the Gulf commonly take somewhere
between 15% and 30% of the order value. For a restaurant running on thin food
margins, that is often the difference between a profitable month and a flat one.

**The customer theft.** The restaurant does not receive the customer's phone
number, name, or order history. A regular who orders every Thursday is invisible
to the kitchen that feeds them. If the restaurant wants that person back next
week, it pays commission again.

**How they cope today.** Three bad options:
1. Accept the commission and shrink the margin.
2. Raise menu prices on the app — which loses them orders.
3. Take orders over WhatsApp by hand: someone reads messages, writes them on
   paper, calls back to confirm. It works until it is busy, which is exactly
   when it fails.

**The cost of doing nothing.** A restaurant doing 30,000 SAR a month in delivery
sales gives away roughly 100,000 SAR a year — and still ends the year not
knowing a single customer's name.

## The Solution

Three surfaces, one product:

**For the diner** — scan the QR on the table or open the restaurant's link. The
menu loads. Order, pay, and watch the status move from *received* to *cooking*
to *ready*. No app to install, no account to create.

**For the kitchen staff** — one screen, today's orders only, newest first. Tap to
move an order forward. Nothing else on the page to get in the way during a rush.

**For the restaurant's own driver** — if the restaurant already has a motorcycle
guy, the owner assigns the order to him. He gets the address on his phone and
one button: *delivered*. MyMenu does not supply drivers and does not run
delivery logistics — it just stops the restaurant from managing that on paper.

**For the owner** — the dashboard. Today's orders, today's revenue, the menu
editor, and the money-saved counter running at the top: *"You kept 8,700 SAR
this month."* Plus the thing no delivery app will ever give them: the list of
their own customers.

## What Makes This Different

**We are not competing with the delivery apps — we are the escape from them.**
Jahez and HungerStation solve discovery: they bring a restaurant strangers.
MyMenu solves retention: it keeps the people who already know the food. A
restaurant can use both, and most should. That honesty is the pitch, not a
weakness in it.

> **"Jahez brings you new people. MyMenu keeps the people you already have."**

**We take the order; we do not drive the food.** Three routes cover the vast
majority of a small restaurant's business — the diner is sitting at the table
(where the delivery apps do not operate at all, and where the restaurant is
losing money to nothing but slow service), the diner collects the order, or the
restaurant sends its own driver. If a customer wants a stranger to drive food
across the city, that is the delivery app's job and we are glad to leave it
there.

**Flat fee, not a percentage.** The moment a restaurant grows, everyone else's
bill grows with it. Ours does not. Our incentives point the same way theirs do.

**The product argues for itself.** The money-saved counter means the owner sees
the value every time they log in. Renewal is not a sales conversation.

**Honest about the moat:** there is no deep technical secret here. The advantage
is execution and focus — being genuinely simple enough that a busy restaurant
owner sets it up in an afternoon, and being priced so plainly that the decision
takes one minute.

## Who This Serves

**Primary: the small independent restaurant owner.** One to three branches. Runs
on delivery apps because there was no alternative. Watches the commission line
every month and can quote it from memory. Not technical — will never read
documentation. Success for them: setup done in one sitting, and a smaller bill.

**Secondary: the kitchen staff.** Needs one screen that works during the rush.
Success: no missed orders, no paper.

**Tertiary: the diner.** Wants food without downloading anything. Success: from
QR scan to order placed in under a minute.

## Success Criteria

**User signals**
- A restaurant completes setup (menu live + QR printed) within 24 hours of signup
- 70% of a restaurant's orders in month three come from returning customers
- Diner completes an order in under 60 seconds from scan

**Business objectives**
- 50 paying restaurants within 6 months → ~15,000 SAR monthly recurring revenue
- Under 5% monthly churn (the money-saved counter is the retention mechanism)
- Cost to sign one restaurant stays under 400 SAR

## Scope

**In, for version one**
- Restaurant signup, login, password reset
- Landing page aimed at restaurant owners
- Menu editor (categories, items, prices, photo, availability toggle)
- Public ordering page per restaurant + printable table QR code
- Three fulfilment modes on every order: **dine-in**, **pickup**, **own driver**
- Live order screen for staff, with status changes
- Assign an order to one of the restaurant's own drivers; driver sees the
  address on his phone and marks it delivered
- Owner dashboard: orders, revenue, **money-saved counter**, customer list
- Account / Settings / Security: profile, opening hours, password, AR/EN, notifications
- Support: FAQs and a contact form that actually sends email

**Explicitly out, for now**
- Supplying drivers, or any delivery logistics of our own — we route the order
  to the restaurant's existing driver and stop there. No live map tracking, no
  route optimisation, no driver marketplace.
- Online card payment — cash and pay-on-collection first
- A native mobile app — the web page is the product
- Multi-branch management — one restaurant, one location, first

**Assumption to verify before the pitch:** the exact commission rate charged
locally. Call two restaurants and ask what percentage they actually pay. A real
quoted number from a real owner is worth more in the room than any published
figure.

## Vision

Start with the ordering page. But once a restaurant's menu, orders, and
customers live in one place, the next things follow naturally: a loyalty card
that actually works because we know who the customers are; a "we miss you"
message to someone who has not ordered in a month; simple stock alerts driven by
what is selling; a shared ordering page for a whole food court or a mall.

In three years, MyMenu is the layer a small restaurant runs on — and the
delivery apps go back to being what they should have been all along: one channel
among several, not the landlord.
